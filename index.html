The 4 stages at a glance

StageWhat you doExample
1 · BasicA simple sentence: subject + verb + objectCars cause pollution.
2 · DetailAdd adjectives and phrases: who, what, where, when, how muchPrivate cars cause serious air pollution in big cities.
3 · ClauseKeep Stage 2 and add a reason or result clausePrivate cars cause serious air pollution in big cities, which harms the health of millions of residents.
4 · AcademicNominalise the subject, hedge the claim, use collocationsThe rapid growth in private car ownership is likely to be a major cause of air pollution in large cities, which poses a serious threat to public health.

Stage 2 · Add detail

  • Who exactly? children → many primary schoolchildren
  • What exactly? phones → smartphones · helps → creates jobs
  • Where / when? in rural areas · during rush hours · over the past five years
  • How much / how often? a lot → for several hours a day
Replace vague words

good, bad, a lot, thing, big, get → a precise noun, verb or fact. No new clause yet.

Stage 3 · Add a reason or result clause

  • Reason: … because + S + V · … as + S + V
  • Result: …, so + S + V · …, which + V (refers to the whole idea before it)
  • Contrast: Although + S + V, …
Punctuation

Comma before so and , which; no comma before because in the middle of a sentence.

Stage 4 · Make it academic

  • Nominalise the subject: people eat fast food → regular consumption of fast food · cities grow → the expansion of cities
  • Hedge the claim: is likely to · may · could · tends to · appears to
  • Use collocations: pose a serious threat to · contribute to · reduce the risk of · public health
Avoid

Over-claiming (always, never, everyone, proves), contractions and spoken words (a lot of, stuff, kids).

One sentence, four stages

  • 1 Fast food is bad.
  • 2 Regular fast-food meals are high in salt, sugar and fat. (how often + what exactly)
  • 3 Regular fast-food meals are high in salt, sugar and fat, so they increase the risk of obesity and heart disease. (+ result clause)
  • 4 Regular consumption of fast food is likely to increase the risk of obesity and heart disease, particularly among young adults. (nominalised subject + hedge)
